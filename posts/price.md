the dsa is slowly hijacking the democratic party, and israel is the instrument. it works because israel is the one issue that unites an otherwise disjointed coalition. it brings in activists, it pulls in some independents, and it hands a fractured left a single rallying cry. the cost of assembling a coalition this way is that it also absorbs genuine antisemites. the people running the strategy treat that absorption as acceptable. that is the phrase that keeps surfacing when you watch how they respond to it: the price of doing business.

## three degrees of separation from antisemitism

the cleanest way to see it is as a spectrum, three degrees of separation from the act itself.

1. **the people committing the acts.** hamas. the october slaughter, the hunting of jews for being jews. unambiguous.
2. **the people supporting the people committing the acts.** the figures and activists who celebrate, justify, or run cover for the actors.
3. **the people who wave it away.** the ones who, at every synagogue attack, every assault on a jew, every wave of hateful rhetoric online and in the streets, say "that's terrible, that's awful," and then change nothing, because the coalition is worth more to them than the pattern is disqualifying. this third tier is what makes the whole machine run.

take zohran mamdani as an illustration of the third tier. read his public record on this and the shape is consistent: israel comes up constantly, and the actor that started the war, hamas, goes unnamed. condemnation stays abstract while the fixation stays specific. that is what the third degree looks like in a politician: a studied refusal to see it plainly.

rashida tlaib is the same shape at the level of a sitting member of congress, and here the record is formal. the house censured her on november 7, 2023, by a vote of 234 to 188, with 22 democrats joining, for "promoting false narratives regarding the october 7, 2023, hamas attack on israel" and for rhetoric read as calling for israel's destruction, the "from the river to the sea" line she has defended as aspirational.[^1][^2] whatever you make of the censure itself, the pattern is the argument: israel is named without end, hamas is never the subject, and the attack that started the war is minimized or reframed on the day it is invoked. that is the third degree, on the floor of the house.

## the double standard is the tell

there are three ds to antisemitism, from natan sharansky: demonization, delegitimization, and double standards. the double standard is the one that catches the most people, because it hides inside what looks like principle.

if you focus intensely on israel while turning a blind eye to everything you would otherwise condemn, that is the tell. declining to attend israel's celebration on the stated principle of opposing "any state with religion as a first-class citizen," while attending the independence celebration of a country founded as an explicitly islamic republic, is a double standard, and by sharansky's own test it points at antisemitism. i would argue it is disqualifying on its own.

## the right is a different picture

the right has antisemitism too, and i won't pretend otherwise. there is the strain you can argue about around figures like tucker carlson, and the fair question of whether some of it fixates on israel and jews.

but the structure on the right is more nuanced. the america first position, and the arguments about withholding arms or military aid to israel, are legitimate, debatable policy questions. there should be room for that discussion.

what there should not be room for is the maximalist set: the "right of return," and the claim that israel should not be a jewish state. those reach into the internal, sovereign affairs of another nation, and they are non-starters. you do not hear the right making those maximalist claims. the activist wing capturing the democratic coalition does, routinely.

## the two-state solution is dead

here is a claim i will make outright, and it is empirical before it is moral: october 7 killed the two-state solution. when a mass slaughter is launched out of the exact territory a future palestinian state would sit on, no israeli government can turn to its public and sell handing over more land to stand up a sovereign, armed neighbor on that border. the entire premise of two-state, land in exchange for security, died that morning, because the trade was shown to run in one direction. you can argue about whether it was ever truly alive. after october 7 it is not, and pretending otherwise is not realism.

so when the demand persists anyway, and especially when it arrives in its maximalist forms, "right of return" and a state that dissolves israel as a jewish state, it stops functioning as a peace proposal. it becomes a permanent grievance to organize a coalition around, which is exactly its political usefulness. the palestinians are not getting a sovereign state in any timeframe that matters, and most of the people loudest about it know that. anyone still insisting on it either is not serious or is not after a state. they are after the fight, because the fight is what keeps the voters together.

## why it keeps growing

this is the engine. as long as the party uses these platforms to unite its voters, it will always have a way to absorb more of them. the underlying issues are not going away, so the absorption never stops. and a meaningful share of the people being absorbed are genuinely antisemitic, many of them islamist, hostile to jews and to anything outside their own frame. the people steering the coalition see all of it as the cost of doing business.

that is the part worth staying awake to. a party does not have to endorse the worst people it recruits. it only has to decide they are worth the votes. once it makes that trade quietly enough, often enough, the character of the coalition changes without anyone ever having to admit it did.
