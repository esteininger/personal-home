- **What:** A **wooden bar/cleat mounted to the DOOR** (ribbed steel. see photo) to **screw hooks/accessories into**, so hung items stop slipping off the bare rib lip. **No factory threaded holes on the door** → can't bolt to captive nuts here.
- **How the Transit community mounts wood to a door with no factory holes. two standard ways:**
  - **A) 3M VHB adhesive. no drilling (recommended for light hook loads).** Bond the cleat with a **perimeter of ¼" 3M VHB tape**; **prime the raw wood first** (contact cement or 3M 90 spray) so it actually sticks. Zero holes → nothing on the exterior, no rust. Popular exactly because it avoids drilling the door.
  - **B) Rivnuts into the door's INNER STRUCTURAL frame. stronger, removable.** Drill fresh holes in the **inner structural rib (NOT the thin outer skin)**, set **M6 / ¼-20 rivnuts**, bolt the cleat on. Handles heavier loads; needs a rivnut tool. ⚠️ Rivnuts in bare sheet-metal pull out under load. go into the structural frame.
  - *(Off-the-shelf: **Flatline Van Co.** makes Transit rear-door panels/platforms if I'd rather buy a kit.)*
- **⚠️ Door cautions:** never drill the **outer skin** (shows outside + rusts) or into **wiring / latch rods / window motor** behind the panel. check depth + what's behind first. The **door slams**, so the mount must take vibration (full VHB perimeter, or ≥2 rivnuts).
- **The rail:** ¾" ply / hardwood strip, sealed/painted; screw hooks / a hook rail into the wood face.
- **Need to buy:** hardwood/ply strip; **3M VHB tape (¼") + primer (contact cement / 3M 90 spray)** *(Option A)*. OR **rivnut tool + M6/¼-20 rivnuts + bolts** *(Option B)*; hooks/hardware; sealant/paint
- **Tools:** drill ✅, oscillating saw ✅, rivnut tool (only for Option B)
- **Measure/decide:** VHB vs rivnuts (load + do you want ZERO holes?); **rail length**; **flat bonding area** (for VHB) or **inner-rib locations + what's behind the skin** (for rivnuts)
