this table compares israel with hamas, hezbollah, the houthis, and iran's irgc across ten categories. every claim links to a source.

israel its adversaries
founding document the 1948 declaration of independence pledges "complete equality of social and political rights to all inhabitants irrespective of religion, race, or sex" and extends peace to neighboring states. the 1988 hamas charter opens: "israel will exist and will continue to exist until islam will obliterate it." article 13: "there is no solution for the palestine question except through jihad."
civilian infrastructure every apartment built after 1993 has a reinforced bomb shelter (mamad) by law. the iron dome intercepts over 90% of rockets aimed at populated areas. the idf found tunnel networks, weapons caches, and military bunkers beneath al-shifa hospital. cnn journalists confirmed a substantial underground structure on the hospital grounds in november 2023.
value of life in 2011, israel exchanged 1,027 prisoners for one captured soldier, gilad shalit. the highest price israel has paid for a single person. hamas kindergarten walls read "the children of the kindergarten are the shahids of tomorrow." textbooks use martyr body counts in arithmetic problems and describe youth deaths as "fuel" for the intifada.
democracy parliamentary democracy with universal suffrage. arab citizens vote and hold knesset seats. freedom house rates israel as "free." hamas won the 2006 palestinian legislative election. no election has been held since. hamas seized full control of gaza in 2007 after armed conflict with fatah.
conduct in war the idf drops leaflets, sends text messages, makes phone calls, and fires warning munitions ("roof knocking") before strikes. urban warfare experts at west point call these measures unprecedented. on october 7, 2023, hamas crossed into israel and killed approximately 1,200 people at a music festival, in kibbutzim, and in their homes. they took roughly 250 hostages.
education 13 nobel laureates. highest R&D spending as share of gdp among oecd nations (6.3%). more tech startups per capita than any country. three universities in the global top 100. palestinian authority textbooks depict jihad as an obligation and promise heavenly rewards for martyrdom. a 2006 survey found 36% of boys aged 12-14 in gaza wanted to become martyrs by age 18.
gay rights decriminalized homosexuality in 1988. tel aviv pride draws over 250,000 attendees, the largest pride event in the middle east. homosexuality is punishable by death in gaza. in 2016, hamas executed a senior commander by firing squad for homosexual activity.
women's rights women serve in over 90% of idf positions. golda meir led the country in 1969, decades before most western nations elected a woman head of state. in gaza, male family members control women under patriarchal personal status laws. nearly a quarter of married people in gaza were married as minors in 2015. honor killings persist.
religious freedom the state recognizes and protects judaism, islam, christianity, the druze faith, and the baha'i faith. the baha'i world centre sits in haifa. hamas has pursued gradual islamization of gaza since 2007. the hamas charter (article 11) declares the land an "islamic waqf" for all generations of muslims.
regional posture peace treaties with egypt (1979) and jordan (1994). abraham accords with the uae, bahrain, and morocco (2020). iran grew hezbollah's rocket arsenal from 12,000 in 2006 to over 150,000. houthis attacked civilian shipping in the red sea, forcing global rerouting around africa.
